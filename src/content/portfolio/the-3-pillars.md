I designed the cover and the interior for *1 Life 3 Pillars: The Champion
Within*, a self-published book by JC Lin about the three areas he argues a life
runs on — health, relationships, and wealth.

Lin's own story is on the back cover, and it is the reason the book exists. He
chased money to the exclusion of everything else, watched his health and his
relationships come apart while his earnings climbed, and then had a serious
health scare that stopped him. He wrote the book so that other people might
avoid pouring themselves into one pillar while the other two quietly collapse.
The argument the book makes is that the three are not separate accounts to be
managed independently — they are linked, and a failure in one drains the other
two.

## The problem

That thesis is a design problem before it is a reading problem, because it has
to be visible before anyone opens the book.

A cover that treats health, relationships, and wealth as three topics — three
icons in a row, say — quietly contradicts the argument, because a row says
these are separate things you could take or leave individually. The book's
whole claim is the opposite: that they hold each other up, and that this is
exactly what the author missed when he was younger. The cover had to carry the
interdependence, not just the list.

There was a second, quieter tension. The book preaches balance, but the
author's history leans hard toward one pillar — wealth is the one that nearly
broke him, and it is the one the back cover gives extra weight, with its own
step-by-step section. The design's job was to hold the three in visible
equilibrium regardless of where the writing's centre of gravity sat.

## The cover

Three rings, interlocking, resting in a pair of cupped open hands.

Interlocking is the entire argument in one move. You cannot lift one ring
without moving the other two; pull on any of them and the others follow. Three
rings sitting side by side would have said *choose*. Three rings linked says
*they come together or not at all*, which is the sentence the book is trying to
teach. They are drawn at equal size and equal weight, one colour each — red for
health, blue for relationships, yellow for wealth — so no pillar out-ranks
another on the cover even though the manuscript has a favourite.

The rings are built rather than borrowed, and each carries a different texture
inside its band — a fine internal pattern that keeps the three from reading as
one object in three colours and gives each pillar a character of its own up
close. The cupped hands beneath them, a stock image, do the other half of the
work: they frame the three as something *held*, a possession that can be
cradled or dropped, rather than a monument that stands on its own. That fits a
book about a man who nearly let his slip through his fingers.

The ground is black with a soft internal glow, which is the register this shelf
expects — a little aspiration, a little light — without tipping into the
crystals-and-mist end of the genre.

## The interior

The three colours are the spine of the whole design, and their real job is
inside the book, not on the front.

Health red, relationships blue, wealth yellow — the same three that name the
rings on the cover — become the interior's navigation. Each pillar is coded to
its colour throughout, so a reader always knows which of the three they are
currently inside without checking a running head. The back cover already
rehearses this: it breaks the contents into three colour-matched columns, one
per pillar, so the book's entire structure is legible in a single glance before
purchase. Inside, that same system organises the reading — the cover's rings,
the back cover's columns, and the interior's section coding are one idea worn
three ways.

It is what turns a self-published book on a much-covered subject into something
that reads as a system rather than a stack of advice. The argument is that the
three pillars are one interlinked whole; the colour running from the front
cover to the last chapter is how the design keeps saying so on every page.

## Scope of work

- **Book cover design** — front cover, spine, and back cover, including the ring illustration and colour system
- **Interior layout design** — full interior, chapter openers, and the three-colour navigation system

The rings on the cover interlock because the book's whole point is that you
cannot hold just one. The design's job, from the front cover to the last page,
was to keep that true where the author's own younger self had gotten it wrong —
to give equal weight, in colour and in space, to the two pillars that are
easiest to drop while chasing the third.
